Ever have the kid who rolls with everything — new schedule, new teacher, last-minute plan change — and then one random Tuesday, over something small, they completely fall apart?

It catches a lot of parents off guard. We tend to save our attention for the kid who's visibly struggling, and the "easy" one learns to keep their feelings tucked away — until they can't anymore.

One thing that helps: build in a low-key emotional check-in before things pile up, not just after a meltdown. At dinner or in the car, try "what was the hardest part of your day?" instead of "how was your day?" It gives them a specific door to walk through, and it tells them big feelings are expected and welcome, not just tolerated when they show up loudly.

Your steady kid still needs somewhere to put the hard stuff. They just may not ask for it the same way.
