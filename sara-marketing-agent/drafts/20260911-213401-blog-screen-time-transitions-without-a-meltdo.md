# Blog Post Concepts: Screen Time Transitions Without a Meltdown

---

## Concept 1

**Working Title:** "Why does my kid melt down when I turn off the screen?"

**Outline:** Explains what's actually happening in that moment — a kid deep in an engaging activity being asked to stop abruptly, without it being framed as a diagnosis-specific issue or a discipline failure. Covers a few concrete tools: warnings before the transition, giving kids a sense of control over *how* the screen time ends, and having a clear next activity ready so the transition isn't just "stop" but "switch to something else." Ends with reassurance that this is a common, fixable pattern, not a sign something is wrong with the child or the parenting.

**Content Pillar:** Big emotions

**Suggested word count:** 800–900 words

---

## Concept 2

**Working Title:** "Screen time warnings that actually work (not just '5 more minutes')"

**Outline:** Digs into why the standard "5 more minutes" warning often fails and offers more effective alternatives — visual timers, countdown routines, letting the child choose the stopping point within a range, and pairing warnings with a preview of what's next. Includes a short troubleshooting section for when warnings still don't land, without pathologizing the behavior. Positions these as tools any family can use, regardless of why transitions are hard for their particular kid.

**Content Pillar:** Routines & structure

**Suggested word count:** 700–800 words

## Concept 3

**Working Title:** "Screen time struggles aren't just an autism/ADHD thing — Austin parent coaching for every kid"

**Outline:** Opens by naming a common assumption — that only kids with a diagnosis need structured screen time strategies — and gently corrects it, pointing out that transitions are hard for lots of kids for lots of reasons (temperament, fatigue, developmental stage, life changes). Closes with an invitation for local parents to reach out, whether or not their child has a formal diagnosis.

**Content Pillar:** All neurotypes welcome (with local Austin SEO angle)
