SUBJECT LINE: The one shift that changes everything

PREVIEW TEXT: It's not about trying harder — it's about trying different

BODY:

Hi there,

I want to tell you about a moment that changes things for almost every parent I work with.

It usually happens a few sessions in. A mom or dad will say something like, "I've tried everything — I just need him to listen better." And we'll dig into what "trying everything" actually looked like: more reminders, bigger consequences, more explaining, more patience stretched thinner and thinner.

Here's the shift: the goal isn't to try harder. It's to try different.

Most of us were raised on the idea that if a strategy isn't working, we should push it harder — more repetition, more firmness, more willpower (theirs and ours). But a strategy that isn't working usually isn't missing effort. It's missing fit.

So instead of asking "how do I get my kid to follow this routine," try asking "what does my kid actually need in order to follow this routine?" Sometimes it's a visual reminder instead of a verbal one. Sometimes it's one step at a time instead of a list. Sometimes it's just five extra minutes of transition time you didn't think you had to give.

That one reframe — from "try harder" to "try different" — takes the pressure off everyone. You're not failing. Your kid isn't being difficult on purpose. You're just looking for the version of the plan that actually fits how your family works.
